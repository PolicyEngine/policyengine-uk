# Screening reported ESA and JSA claims

Income-related ESA and income-based JSA remain bounded models of reported
awards, rather than complete entitlement calculations. The original claimant
and partner share one reported claim. Each member outside that couple who
reports an award has a separate claim: their own existing work screen applies,
and tariff income is deducted separately. The benefit unit's capital remains
an explicit proxy because capital owned by each outside claimant is not observed.

For example, using ordinary capital of £7,000 and the model's 52-week convention,
tariff income is £208 per claim. A couple reporting £3,000 and two outside people
reporting £1,000 and £500 receive £2,792 + £792 + £292 = £3,876 if every existing
screen passes. The couple's part stays £2,792. A working outside reporter cannot
raise the total; failure of the couple's own screen cannot erase another eligible
person's separate award. These examples presume separate preassessed claims.

All formula readers use the same original claim groups. A scope mask selects
reports; it does not change claimant or partner roles. Claimant-or-partner awards
exclude outside claims, including in Income Support and an alternate benefit-cap
couple. Formula provenance compares the complete and scoped screened amounts
using the existing stored precision and half-penny convention. Raw-total and
arbitrary entered or reformed value fallbacks, explicit scoped inputs and
neutralisation stay unchanged. Equal input and formula values remain
indistinguishable by design.

The 16–19 Bursary Fund own-right ESA signal now reads payable-person IR-ESA status
or the person's contributory ESA value. Contributory ESA itself and the unchanged
UC limb retain reported-benefit proxies; an explicit signal input remains
authoritative. This helper does not certify full ESA entitlement or an
institution's bursary decision. Under DfE's 2026–27 guide, section 3.1, students
aged 19 or over cannot enter the defined vulnerable groups, including continuers
and students with an EHC plan. An older student's continuing ESA can test the
helper only, rather than establish a vulnerable-group grant.

The existing annual closure, 52-week and shared-capital approximations remain.
No population estimate or absence of outside survey reports is claimed.

The relevant rules are the ESA Regulations 2008, regulations 110 and 118; JSA
Regulations 1996, regulations 107 and 116; Welfare Reform Act 2007, Schedule 1,
paragraph 6; Jobseekers Act 1995, sections 1 and 3; and the DfE 16–19 Bursary Fund
guide for 2026–27, sections 3.1, 4.3 and 4.4. The existing variable references
link to those provisions.
