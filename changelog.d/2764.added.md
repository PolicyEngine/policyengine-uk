Add `trading_allowance_claim_status`, a person input saying whether the input data's self-employment profit already deducts the trading allowance (claimant), deducts actual expenses above it (non-claimant), or is unknown (the default, which keeps the current treatment). A claimant's receipts are their profit plus the allowance legislated for the year the data were measured (a dataset's first year, or the latest year a situation supplied the status for), which reforms do not move, so a raise to any level relieves them by its excess over that allowance, up to their profit; their capital allowances, which partial relief replaces, are not deducted. Their expenses are a share of that allowance, set by `gov.simulation.trading_allowance_claimant_expense_share` (between 0 and 1, by default 1): they keep the allowance, giving up current losses, while it is above nil and at least those expenses, and otherwise deduct them, so a cut adds by default nothing. A non-claimant gets the allowance only through full relief, where supplied receipts, or casual income alone with no profit, are within it. Income tax and Class 4 both read the result, so datasets without turnover can cost changes to the allowance, such as the Budget 2026 raise to £3,000, on the traders who already claim it.
