Add `trading_allowance_claim_status`, a person input saying whether the input data's self-employment profit already deducts the trading allowance (claimant), deducts actual expenses above it (non-claimant), or is unknown (the default, which keeps the current treatment). A claimant's receipts are their profit plus the allowance legislated for the year the data were measured (a dataset's first year, or the year simulated without one), which reforms do not move, so a raise to any level relieves them by its excess over that allowance, up to their profit; their capital allowances, which partial relief replaces, are not deducted, and they keep the allowance, giving up current losses, at any level above nil. A cut below that allowance adds to their profit what `gov.simulation.trading_allowance_claimant_expense_share` sets, by default nothing. A non-claimant gets the allowance only through full relief, where supplied receipts, or casual income alone with no profit, are within it. Income tax and Class 4 both read the result, so datasets without turnover can cost changes to the allowance, such as the Budget 2026 raise to £3,000, on the traders who already claim it.
