- Made a retention-rate elasticity of 1.0 the default capital gains realisation response (`gov.simulation.capital_gains_responses.elasticity`, previously 0), the central value Advani, Lonsdale and Summers (2024, pp. 36-37) use, with 0.5 and 2.0 as sensitivities: CGT reforms are no longer costed statically unless the elasticity is set to 0, and gains qualifying for Business Asset Disposal Relief take the same 1.0. An `mtr_elasticity` set while the retention-rate settings keep their defaults now applies alone in its periods, with a `CapitalGainsResponseWarning`; retention-rate settings changed to nonzero values still raise alongside it. Simulations that apply their baseline's rules (any simulation without a reform, and a reform's own baseline) and populations without gains no longer measure marginal rates on gains, and marginal tax rates on earnings hold realised gains fixed instead of measuring the response again at the higher pay. `marginal_tax_rate_on_capital_gains` now measures the rise in capital gains tax rather than the fall in household net income, which gives the same rate at about a sixth of the cost. `capital_gains_response_assumption` reports the response a simulation applies in a year.
