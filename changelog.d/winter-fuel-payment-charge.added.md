- Add the winter fuel payment charge (`winter_fuel_payment_charge`, ITEPA 2003 s.681I, from 2025-26) with parameters under `gov.hmrc.income_tax.charges.winter_fuel_payment`. It is charged person by person: the whole Winter Fuel Payment (England, Wales and Northern Ireland) or Pension Age Winter Heating Payment, when the person's own total income (ITA 2007 s.23 Step 1) exceeds £35,000 and they are not entitled to Income Support, income-based JSA, Pension Credit, income-related ESA or Universal Credit. It is added to `income_tax` after tax reductions (ITA 2007 s.23 Step 7).
- Add `income_tax_before_winter_fuel_payment_charge`. The Pension Credit, Income Support, Housing Benefit (applicable income and net earnings) and Council Tax Reduction means tests now deduct it instead of `income_tax`. The charge recovers a payment rather than taxing the income those tests assess, and it depends on Pension Credit and Universal Credit receipt, so deducting it would make the model circular. Universal Credit earned income and the benefit cap's earnings test already deduct only each person's own tax on earnings, which leaves out the charge. The property allowance election still compares `income_tax`, which now includes the charge: a route can change total income and Pension Credit, and so whether the charge applies. While the charge has effect, the election compares tax on both routes even for landlords without finance costs, and landlords decide one per household rather than one per benefit unit, because a couple's Pension Credit can change another benefit unit's shared payment and so its charge.
- Add `total_income_charged_to_tax`, total income as ITA 2007 s.23 Step 1 defines it: the model's taxable income components (after employment expenses, capital allowances, the trading and property allowances and exempt ISA interest), less employee pension contributions relieved under a net pay arrangement (`net_pay_pension_relief`, new: FA 2004 s.193(2) deducts them from employment income), with losses brought forward added back. The winter fuel payment charge tests it, and `adjusted_total_income` now reads it for Step 1 instead of computing it itself, deducting at Steps 3 and 4 only the relieved contributions not already deducted, so its values are unchanged. `total_income` still sums gross income.
