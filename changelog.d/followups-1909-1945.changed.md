Note on #1909's council tax reduction capital-route loss, which hits savings-credit-only pensioners whose Pension Credit capital passes £16,000 while their household savings do not: in that PR's dataset run it rested on the `corporate_wealth` proxy, which #1837 and #1936 describe as mostly imputed pension wealth. Pension Credit's capital sources count `corporate_wealth` but not `private_pension_wealth`, and `council_tax_reduction_assessable_capital` uses Pension Credit's figure for these pensioners. In datasets built before the `private_pension_wealth` split, `corporate_wealth` also holds the imputed pension wealth, which Pension Credit disregards (State Pension Credit Regulations 2002 Sch V paras 22 and 23). That loss is an artefact of the proxy, so council tax reduction offsets in pension costings that rest on it should not be read as real effects; a benefit unit that records `pension_credit_reported_capital` bypasses the proxy. Two council tax reduction test cases pin this. Also add a Housing Benefit regression case for #1945: a savings-credit-only pensioner built from age, pensions, rent, tenure and capital alone, with no disregard or income override.
