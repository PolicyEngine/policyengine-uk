- Fix the `disable_simulated_benefits` reform (`gov.contrib.policyengine.disable_simulated_benefits`), which raised an error before setting any benefit. The simulation now links itself to its tax-benefit system before applying structural reforms, so a reform that sets inputs can reach it (the public service budget adjustments failed on the same missing link); a clone or branch that applies a reform links the system to itself while it does, so the reform never sets its parent's inputs. A reform applied after the simulation is built (one passed as `reform=`, or applied to a built simulation, clone or branch) now keeps through `reset_calculations` (and, before policyengine-core 3.33.0, `derivative`) every input it stores and leaves in place, however it stores it, including one that replaces an input set since the build; an input set since the build that the reform did not store is still reset, as before, unless the reform stores an input of the same variable (a reset keeps or clears whole variables). The reform reads the dataset year whether it is stored as text or a number. It no longer lists Attendance Allowance, DLA and PIP, which have no reported amount: the data gives their receipt as a rate category and the model pays that category's rate.
- Under `disable_simulated_benefits`, set the claimant's and partner's income-related ESA and income-based JSA (`claimant_or_partner_esa_income`, `claimant_or_partner_jsa_income`) for each year from the dataset-year reports of whoever is the claimant or partner that year, and set Income Support from their reports alone. The reform holds each benefit at its dataset-year amount while the reported amounts are uprated, so in a later year the stored award matched neither reading of the reports and another member's award was taken to be the claimant's. Another member's own Income Support report is left out of household income, as it is with the reform off, where Income Support is calculated only for the claimant's family.
- Under `disable_simulated_benefits`, no family is moved off its legacy benefits: the reform holds `legacy_benefits_closed` false for its ten years, since it pays every reported legacy award and Universal Credit in every year. So, after the closure of income-based JSA and Income Support from April 2026, an adult outside the couple is still on the income-related ESA or income-based JSA the reform pays them, and the closure ends no family's Housing Benefit or tax credit eligibility and prompts no Universal Credit claim. Only the closure is held: the model's other rules still apply, so a family the model has claim Universal Credit anyway is still not eligible for Housing Benefit or tax credits, though the reform pays the amounts it reports.
- Neutralizing `esa_income` or `jsa_income` also neutralizes the claimant's and partner's award, so an award entered for them, or set by the reform, no longer outlives the benefit it is part of.
- Read the claimant's and partner's income-related ESA and income-based JSA in `income_support_eligible` from `claimant_or_partner_esa_income` and `claimant_or_partner_jsa_income`, as the means tests and passports do, so an award entered for them directly, or set by the reform, is read the same way everywhere. The two variables share one formula, which now treats an award within half a penny of zero as no award, as `income_support_eligible` did, so the float residual left by reports that exactly match the tariff income is no longer the claimant's or partner's award, and no passport or status that reads their award counts it. A member outside the couple is still on their own award as before: it is read from their own report, without that rule. When `esa_income` or `jsa_income` holds the plain total of the reported awards, Income Support now reads the plain total of the claimant's and partner's reports, as the two variables already did; it used to read their award after the capital test.
