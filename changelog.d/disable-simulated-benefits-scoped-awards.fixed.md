- Fix the `disable_simulated_benefits` reform (`gov.contrib.policyengine.disable_simulated_benefits`), which raised an error before setting any benefit. The simulation now links itself to its tax-benefit system before applying structural reforms, so a reform that sets inputs can reach it (the public service budget adjustments failed on the same missing link). The reform reads the dataset year whether it is stored as text or a number. It no longer lists Attendance Allowance, DLA and PIP, which have no reported amount: the data gives their receipt as a rate category and the model pays that category's rate.
- Under `disable_simulated_benefits`, set the claimant's and partner's income-related ESA and income-based JSA (`claimant_or_partner_esa_income`, `claimant_or_partner_jsa_income`) for each year from their own reports in the dataset year, and set Income Support from their reports alone. The reform holds each benefit at its dataset-year amount while the reported amounts are uprated, so in a later year the stored award matched neither reading of the reports and another member's award was taken to be the claimant's.
- Read the claimant's and partner's income-related ESA and income-based JSA in `income_support_eligible` from `claimant_or_partner_esa_income` and `claimant_or_partner_jsa_income`, as the means tests and passports do, so an award entered for them directly, or set by the reform, is read the same way everywhere. The two variables share one formula. When `esa_income` or `jsa_income` holds the plain total of the reported awards, Income Support now reads the plain total of the claimant's and partner's reports, as the two variables already did; it used to read their award after the capital test.
